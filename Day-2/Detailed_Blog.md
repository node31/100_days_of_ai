## Introduction

On Day 1, we learned about Artificial Intelligence and its impact on our world. Today, we’ll focus on Machine Learning (ML)—the driving force behind most modern AI systems.

## What is Machine Learning?

Machine Learning is a field of AI that gives computers the ability to learn from data and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed for each task. Instead of hardcoding rules, we provide data and let the machine find patterns and relationships.

## Types of Machine Learning
- **Supervised Learning:** The model is trained on labeled data (input-output pairs). Example: Email spam detection.
- **Unsupervised Learning:** The model finds patterns in unlabeled data. Example: Grouping customers by purchasing behavior.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** The model learns by interacting with an environment, receiving rewards or penalties. Example: AI playing chess or video games.

## Real-World Examples
- Netflix recommending shows based on your viewing history
- Email services filtering out spam
- Voice assistants understanding your speech
- Self-driving cars recognizing pedestrians and traffic signs

## Why is ML Important?
Machine Learning is the backbone of many AI applications. It allows systems to adapt, improve, and handle complex tasks that would be impossible to program manually. ML is also the foundation for advanced AI topics like LLMs, RAG, and agents.

## How Does ML Work?
1. **Collect Data:** Gather relevant data for the problem.
2. **Train a Model:** Use algorithms to find patterns in the data.
3. **Test & Improve:** Evaluate the model’s performance and refine it.
4. **Deploy:** Use the model to make predictions on new data.
